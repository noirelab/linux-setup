# Research Gap Analysis

Identifies what the knowledge base is missing: topics with thin coverage, open questions
that remain unanswered, and sources worth seeking — without ever inventing evidence.

## When to use

- Reviewing a health-check report's coverage-gap findings.
- Deciding what to research or collect next for a knowledge base.
- Drafting research questions for a client, project, or expert knowledge base.

## Procedure

1. Read the knowledge base focus (`config.yaml`) and the wiki index.
2. Inventory coverage per focus topic: how many pages, how many sources, how recent.
3. Read `system/questions.md` — open questions are direct evidence of missing knowledge.
4. Identify gaps: focus topics with no page, sources never represented in the wiki,
   contradictions with no resolution evidence.
5. For each gap, state: what is missing, why it matters, and what source types could fill it
   (e.g. primary documents, interviews, transcripts, articles, data exports).
6. Produce research questions phrased so an answer would be verifiable against a source.

## Rules

- Never invent missing evidence or fabricate sources.
- Gaps are reported as recommendations, not facts about the world.
- When a gap matters enough, record the question in `system/questions.md` and optionally
  write a proposal suggesting the new page — for human approval.
